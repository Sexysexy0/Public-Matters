Genesis Manifesto

Introduction
We stand at a moment where technology can either concentrate power or restore it. This manifesto chooses restoration. We believe in systems that are legible, accountable, and designed to serve people rather than extract from them.

Core Beliefs
- Human dignity is nonnegotiable.
- Public infrastructure must be governed for the long term.
- Openness and verifiability are prerequisites for trust.
- Participation is not a privilege but a design requirement.

Principles
1. Transparency
All core rules, data schemas, and governance decisions should be auditable and accessible.

2. Inclusive Governance
Decision processes must include diverse voices and lower barriers to meaningful participation.

3. Durable Stewardship
Resources and institutions should be managed to preserve value across generations.

4. Measured Impact
We prioritize outcomes that improve wellbeing, resilience, and equitable opportunity.

Actions
- Anchor canonical documents and version history on public, verifiable storage.
- Publish governance proposals, votes, and rationale in machine‑readable formats.
- Build tooling and educational resources to onboard new participants.
- Establish funding mechanisms that align incentives with long‑term public value.

Conclusion
This is a living manifesto. We invite critique, iteration, and collective stewardship. By anchoring our commitments and measuring our impact, we aim to build institutions that endure and uplift.
