# MSME Technology Readiness and Resilience Framework

## Purpose

This framework helps micro, small, and medium enterprises, support institutions, researchers, and program designers identify an appropriate intervention for a documented business constraint.

It is designed to prevent a one-size-fits-all approach. Automation may be useful for some enterprises, while others may first need cost-shock resilience, process controls, training, inventory discipline, working-capital support, energy efficiency, safety improvements, logistics, market access, or basic digital tools.

This framework does not approve funding, create an entitlement, replace financial advice, or authorize any government program.

## Core principle

Match the intervention to the verified bottleneck, enterprise capacity, and expected user or worker benefit. Do not treat technology acquisition, public visibility, or novelty as proof that an intervention is appropriate.

## Intervention cycle

Identify constraint -> Establish baseline -> Assess readiness -> Select right-sized intervention -> Implement safely -> Measure outcomes -> Review, adapt, scale, or exit

## Enterprise constraints

| Constraint | Common signal | Intervention to evaluate first |
|---|---|---|
| Repetitive high-volume production | Capacity bottleneck, inconsistent output, worker strain | Process redesign, quality controls, automation or robotics where viable |
| Safety or ergonomic risk | Repeated injury risk, hazardous manual task | Safety controls, training, protective equipment, equipment redesign |
| LPG or input-cost shock | Margin compression, smaller portions, reduced purchasing power | Cost-shock continuity plan, energy efficiency, working-capital or verified assistance pathway |
| Inventory loss or stockouts | Spoilage, expired goods, missed high-demand items | Demand tracking, inventory controls, procurement planning |
| Quality inconsistency | Rework, rejects, customer complaints | Standard operating procedures, training, measurement tools, targeted equipment |
| Cash-flow instability | Credit dependence, inability to replenish stock | Cost tracking, payment scheduling, working-capital assessment, support referral |
| Market-access constraint | Limited buyers, unreliable orders, high delivery cost | Product standards, procurement linkages, logistics, digital ordering |
| Skills gap | Underused equipment, error rates, unsafe operation | Training, mentoring, documentation, supervised pilot |
| Information gap | Unknown costs, no sales or capacity baseline | Basic bookkeeping, workflow mapping, demand and cost records |

## Readiness assessment

Before selecting an intervention, document:

1. The specific constraint and who experiences its impact.
2. Current workflow, capacity, safety conditions, costs, and revenue pattern.
3. Existing equipment, skills, maintenance capability, and operating environment.
4. Expected costs, financing or repayment obligations, and downside risks.
5. Required permits, standards, utility needs, connectivity, space, and training.
6. A realistic outcome measure and review period.
7. A lower-cost, lower-risk alternative if the proposed intervention fails.

## Right-sized intervention ladder

Consider options from least complex to more capital-intensive:

1. Workflow clarification, recordkeeping, or operating procedure.
2. Training, mentoring, safety improvements, and maintenance.
3. Simple tools, quality controls, inventory methods, or energy-efficiency improvements.
4. Shared services, cooperative procurement, logistics, or digital-ordering support.
5. Targeted equipment upgrade with training and maintenance plan.
6. Automation or robotics where production volume, safety conditions, skills, and financing capacity justify it.

A higher-cost option should not be selected only because it is more visible, modern, or easier to showcase.

## Selection criteria

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Need fit | Does the intervention address the documented bottleneck? |
| Affordability | Can the enterprise sustain purchase, maintenance, utilities, and repayment? |
| Worker benefit | Does it improve safety, workload, skills, income stability, or job quality? |
| Operational readiness | Are space, utilities, training, repair, and supervision available? |
| Market fit | Is there demand sufficient to justify expanded capacity? |
| Inclusion | Can micro-enterprises, women-led businesses, informal-to-formal transitions, and rural enterprises reasonably access support? |
| Risk | What happens if demand, costs, equipment, or financing assumptions fail? |
| Evidence | Can outcomes be measured without collecting unnecessary personal data? |

## Outcome categories

Measure outcomes against a documented baseline where possible:

- Productivity or throughput
- Quality and rework
- Safety and ergonomic exposure
- Energy or input consumption
- Inventory availability, spoilage, and stockouts
- Revenue stability and cash-flow resilience
- Worker skills and operating confidence
- Customer fulfillment and market access
- Maintenance burden and downtime
- Unintended effects, including debt stress, exclusion, displacement, or waste

## Review and adaptation

At the review date:

1. Compare observed outcomes against the baseline and expected outcome.
2. Identify whether the constraint was correctly diagnosed.
3. Record worker, owner, customer, and affected-community feedback where appropriate.
4. Continue, adapt, scale, pause, or exit based on evidence.
5. Publish only non-sensitive aggregate lessons where a public-support program is involved.

## Equity and safeguards

- Do not require a microenterprise to adopt expensive technology merely to qualify for support.
- Do not represent a pilot success as proof that an intervention works everywhere.
- Do not use enterprise data beyond the stated assessment and review purpose.
- Do not publish business-sensitive details without authorization.
- Do not displace workers without planning for safety, training, redeployment, or transition support.
- Keep assistance criteria understandable, documented, and open to review.

## Limits

This framework does not replace engineering assessment, occupational-safety requirements, accounting review, lending due diligence, procurement rules, labor protections, environmental requirements, legal obligations, or formal program eligibility criteria.
