# Digital Access Restriction Decision Framework

## Purpose

This framework helps public institutions, service providers, researchers, and affected communities evaluate whether a restriction on access to an online platform or service is necessary, proportionate, time-limited, evidence-based, and subject to review.

It is a governance and documentation framework. It does not authorize blocking, surveillance, content removal, or any action that requires legal authority.

## Core principle

Restrictions on access to digital services should be exceptional. The response should target the documented harm as precisely as possible while minimizing unnecessary disruption to lawful speech, work, education, community activity, accessibility, and private communication.

## Scope

This framework applies to proposed or actual restrictions affecting access to online platforms, communication services, collaboration tools, marketplaces, forums, social networks, or similar digital services.

## Definitions

| Term | Meaning in this framework |
|---|---|
| Access restriction | A measure that limits availability, reach, functionality, or connectivity to a digital service |
| Targeted measure | A measure directed at identified unlawful content, accounts, behavior, or technical endpoints |
| Platform-wide restriction | A measure affecting a broad service or a large user population |
| Evidence record | A dated, attributable record supporting a factual claim or decision |
| Review | A documented reassessment of necessity, proportionality, scope, and outcomes |

## Decision standard

A proposed restriction should not proceed unless all of the following questions can be answered and documented:

1. What specific harm is alleged or documented?
2. What evidence supports the claim, and what is its quality?
3. What legal authority and procedural safeguards apply?
4. What connection exists between the documented harm and the proposed technical measure?
5. What less-restrictive alternatives were considered and why are they inadequate?
6. Who is likely to be affected, including workers, students, educators, communities, and people relying on accessibility tools?
7. What is the narrowest technical and geographic scope that could address the documented harm?
8. What is the duration, review date, notice process, and termination condition?
9. What appeal, correction, or redress path exists for affected parties?
10. How will outcomes and unintended harms be measured and reported?

## Least-restrictive-means ladder

Consider responses in this order before a broad access restriction:

1. Preserve and verify relevant evidence.
2. Report or remove specific unlawful content through applicable procedures.
3. Restrict identified accounts, channels, servers, domains, or technical endpoints where lawful and technically justified.
4. Require a documented safety response, point of contact, or compliance process from the responsible service provider.
5. Use targeted, time-limited technical measures subject to notice and review.
6. Consider a platform-wide restriction only when narrower measures are documented as insufficient and the required legal safeguards are satisfied.

## Evidence quality

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `observed` | Directly documented by a responsible investigator or system record |
| `reported` | Attributed to a named source but not independently verified in the record |
| `reproduced` | Independently confirmed through documented method |
| `disputed` | Credibly challenged, incomplete, or contested |
| `unsupported` | Insufficient information for reliance |

A serious restriction should not rely solely on unsupported or unattributed claims.

## Necessity and proportionality test

Document the following before and during a restriction:

| Test | Required question |
|---|---|
| Legitimate objective | Is there a defined and lawful safety, rights-protection, or public-interest objective? |
| Evidence connection | Does evidence connect the harm to the proposed measure? |
| Suitability | Is the measure reasonably capable of reducing the documented harm? |
| Necessity | Is a less-restrictive option inadequate on the documented facts? |
| Proportionality | Do expected benefits outweigh foreseeable harm to lawful users and services? |
| Time limitation | Does the measure have a clear expiry, review date, and termination condition? |
| Accountability | Are the decision-maker, authority, evidence basis, and review path identified? |

## Notice, transparency, and review

Where legally and operationally possible:

- Give advance notice to affected service providers and users.
- State the legal authority, stated objective, technical scope, start time, end time, and review date.
- Publish a non-sensitive summary of the evidence and alternatives considered.
- Provide a contact point for corrections, emergency continuity concerns, and appeal.
- Maintain a public or independently reviewable decision record.
- Publish an outcome report after termination, renewal, or material change.

## Continuity protections

Before imposing a broad restriction, identify and mitigate foreseeable disruption to:

- Schools, universities, educators, and student groups
- Workers, freelancers, developers, and remote teams
- Public-interest communities and volunteer networks
- Accessibility and assistive-technology users
- Crisis communication, safety, and support channels
- Essential records, project coordination, and legitimate business operations

See [Digital Work and Education Continuity Checklist](../research/digital-work-education-continuity-checklist.md).

## Post-action evaluation

At the review date, document:

1. Whether the stated harm decreased and how that was measured.
2. Whether the measure caused unintended disruption.
3. Whether a narrower measure could now replace the restriction.
4. Whether continuation remains necessary and proportionate.
5. What policy, safety, investigative, or service-provider improvements should follow.

## Limits

This framework does not replace constitutional protections, judicial review, statutory requirements, platform policy, technical expertise, independent oversight, or affected-community consultation.
